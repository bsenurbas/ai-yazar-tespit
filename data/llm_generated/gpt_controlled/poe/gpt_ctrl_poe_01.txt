Toward the close of an autumnal evening, when the vapors from the marsh had crept, with a noiseless and almost sentient malignity, into the single crooked street of that remote township, there appeared at the northern road a solitary stranger, whose advent, though unattended by cry, bell, or hoofbeat, seemed to press upon the drowsing houses with the weight of an omen. He was tall, much bent, and enveloped in a cloak of such funereal darkness that the very lamps, swinging before the tavern and the shuttered apothecary, appeared to shrink from casting their sickly radiance upon him; and as he advanced, slowly, yet without the hesitation of one who seeks direction, the few inhabitants who watched from behind their warped panes felt, each in the secrecy of his own breast, that this man had not come by chance, nor for commerce, nor for shelter, but as the visible consequence of some ancient and unconfessed transgression.

The town itself, long severed from the great roads by forest, fen, and a range of barren hills whose summits were seldom free from cloud, possessed that peculiar air of decay which does not spring from poverty alone, but from a spiritual exhaustion, a surrender of hope so gradual that no living resident could recall its beginning. The church leaned upon its stones like an old sinner upon a crutch; the inn door, swollen by many winters, groaned before the hand that opened it; and the faces within, pallid in the smoky glow, turned toward the entrant with that mingled curiosity and dread which attends the intrusion of a dream into waking life. No one addressed him at first, for his eyes, though calm, had in them a depth too deliberate to be merely human in its patience; they seemed to have looked long upon sufferings not their own, and to have found in them neither pity nor surprise.

When at last he spoke, his voice was low, precise, and mournfully courteous, yet every syllable fell with an unnatural distinctness, as if pronounced not into the common air but into the private conscience of every hearer; and the room, already oppressed by storm and midnight, became at once a tribunal, though no accusation had been named. He asked only for lodging, and for knowledge of a house that stood, he said, beyond the mill-stream, where the alder trees grew thickest and the ground was soft underfoot; but at this modest inquiry several men lowered their gaze, one woman crossed herself with trembling haste, and the landlord, whose lips had been red with drink a moment before, grew gray about the mouth. Then it was understood—though none dared say how it had been understood—that the stranger had come not to discover what lay hidden in the town, but to remind the town that nothing hidden is ever dead!
