They found me at dawn with the window open and the rain laid gently across my hands, as though the weather itself had come to close what no human hand could mend. Those who entered spoke in hushed voices, mistaking stillness for tragedy, and perhaps they were not wrong; yet I, if I may still call myself I, stood nowhere near the bed. I was in the rain.

Not as a ghost, no—there was no chain, no corridor, no pale tribunal of the wronged—but as a loosened attention, a final listening. I heard the city wake: shutters lifted, hooves striking stone, bread broken, names called across courtyards. Every sound entered me and passed through without becoming a room. There was no door hidden beneath it. There was only life, brief and unpossessed, moving onward with its grave, indifferent grace.

When they closed my eyes, I saw at last the old house—not as ruin, not as judge, but as a shape in distant fog, growing smaller while morning gathered behind it. At an upper window stood my brother, not waiting, not accusing. He raised his hand once, and the fog took him. Then even the memory of the house dissolved, and in the vast quiet that followed, I heard neither knocking nor key, but rain falling upon open ground.
