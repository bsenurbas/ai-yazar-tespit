I stood above that little door while the morning, only moments before so merciful, withdrew from the field as though ashamed to witness what followed. The grass about the threshold blackened in a perfect circle; the dew became ink; and the birds, those careless heralds of ordinary life, fell silent in the hedges. I might have cast the key away, but my hand had closed around it with the devotion of a fist around a wound.

The knocking continued, gentle as a child asking leave to enter. Yet I knew—O, how deeply I knew!—that it came not from below, but from within some chamber of myself that the house had merely taught me to hear. I knelt, and as the key entered the lock, the field vanished, the road vanished, the sky itself folded inward like a curtain drawn over a sickbed. Behind the door was no stair, no vault, no nursery. There was only a desk, a candle, and a sheet of paper upon which a hand was writing my story in my brother’s name.

When I bent to read it, the candle flame leaned toward me, thin and eager, and the words rose from the page like insects from a grave. They told of a man who escaped a haunted house, crossed a gate, found a key, and opened a door in the earth. They told all this with such cruel fidelity that I reached the final line before I understood its meaning. There, in fresh black ink, it said: He believed the house had ended, because he had not yet learned that a tale, once opened, is another kind of room.
