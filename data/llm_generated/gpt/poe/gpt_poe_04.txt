The corridor lengthened as I walked, though I swear upon the dust of my name that my feet scarcely moved; the doors slid past me in mournful procession, each one breathing out a different sorrow, until the air itself became crowded with grief. From behind one came the sound of a woman weeping into silk; from another, the scratch of a pen dragged across paper by a hand no longer flesh; from a third, a laugh so thin and brittle that it shattered before it reached me.

My brother did not retreat, nor did he advance. He merely waited beneath the arch of the last doorway, patient as a sentence already passed, while the letter in his hand unfolded and unfolded, impossibly, until it trailed along the floor like a winding-sheet. Upon it were written not words, but scenes: my childhood, my envy, the quiet cruelties I had named discipline, the silence I had mistaken for absolution. I saw myself in every line—and in every line, I was smaller.

At last he raised one finger to his lips, and the house obeyed. The whispers ceased; the walls stilled; even my heart, that treacherous prisoner, seemed to pause in expectation. Then the final door opened behind me, though I had not heard it appear, and from within came a warm, familiar darkness. My brother stepped aside. I entered. And the door closed with the soft, satisfied sound of a book being shut forever.
