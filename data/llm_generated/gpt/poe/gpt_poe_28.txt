The rain fell for a long while, though time had ceased to be counted in hours and had become instead a widening circle upon the earth. Each drop struck the ground and vanished into it without protest, and I understood, in that wordless descent, the dignity denied to so many souls: to enter darkness without being locked there, to be received without becoming a secret. No house rose around me. No hand wrote my name upon a door.

Yet from far below, or far within, there came once a sound so faint that it might have been only the memory of sound: not the cradle, not the key, not the pleading of the forgotten, but a child’s laugh, brief and astonished, as if some ancient room had opened at last upon a field. I listened, if listening remained to me, and felt no terror. The laugh rose through rain, through soil, through morning, and was gone before grief could claim it.

Then all things grew simple. Stone was stone. Wood was wood. The dead were not dismissed, but they no longer stood waiting at every threshold. Above the open ground, the clouds thinned, and a pale light spread across the world with no promise beyond itself. In that light I faded—not as punishment, nor as pardon, but as mist leaves a meadow when the day has need of it no more.
