The page trembled beneath my gaze, though no wind moved in that room, and the hand that wrote—my brother’s hand, my hand, the hand of some patient author buried beneath us all—did not pause. I saw then that ink was only another darkness given discipline, and that every sentence, once formed, became a corridor from which the mind could not wholly return. The candle burned lower, but its flame cast no shadow of me upon the wall; it cast instead the shadow of the house.

I tried to tear the paper, but the sheet yielded like skin and healed beneath my fingers. I tried to overturn the desk, but its legs rooted deep into the unseen floor, drawing nourishment, perhaps, from the very graves I had escaped. The words continued, line after line, calmly recording my defiance before I committed it, my terror before it reached my heart, my silence before it closed my throat. I was not being watched. I was being drafted.

At last the hand stopped. The room listened. Upon the final blank space, a single sentence appeared without the aid of pen or pulse: To leave, write the next door. I lifted the quill, though I knew no salvation had ever begun so politely, and as its point touched the page, the walls drew nearer with a lover’s patience. I wrote one word—home—and somewhere beyond the candlelight, a lock turned.
