When morning entered, it did not blaze; it unfolded, slow and pale, across the room, touching first the window glass, then the chair, then my brother’s face, until he seemed less a visitor than a shape remembered by the light. I wished to ask whether he had suffered, whether the house had loosened its hold, whether the child within him had ever found a field wide enough for play; but my tongue, old servant of fear, hesitated at the gate of speech.

He answered without my asking. “Not all wounds demand repayment,” he said, and the words entered me with the cold mercy of water poured over fever. “Some ask only that they not be made useful.” Then I understood the last vanity of remorse: how eagerly the guilty make monuments of their sorrow, how they polish regret until it resembles virtue, how they call their own suffering a kind of justice while the wounded remain unnamed.

I closed my eyes, and when I opened them, he was gone. In his place, upon the chair, lay the folded paper, now blank on both sides. The street below had begun its day: wheels on stone, voices, a vendor calling bread into the light. I rose slowly, not redeemed, not forgiven, but emptied of the need to be either; and as the first bell rang across the roofs, I placed the blank paper upon the fire and watched it burn without a whisper.
