The men of the expedition did not find ruins on the lunar surface. They found scaffolding. It rose in silver arcs across a crater wall, too vast for any human purpose and too recent to be dismissed as geology. Dust lay upon it, but not deeply.

Captain Renshaw’s report was written with admirable restraint. The beams were hollow, the joints flexible, and the whole structure seemed designed not to stand against gravity but to unfold in its absence. There were marks upon the lower plates where tools, or limbs serving as tools, had passed repeatedly.

The discovery changed the meaning of the moon. It was no longer a dead companion, no longer a lamp hung conveniently above our nights. It was a work site temporarily abandoned. Men looked up thereafter not with romance, but with the uneasy feeling one has on entering a room and finding another man’s coat still warm upon the chair.
