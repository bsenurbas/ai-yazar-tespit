The green men were first observed at dawn by a nursemaid, who reported that three small figures were digging in the wet ground near the ponds. They were not children. They stood scarcely four feet high, with smooth greenish skin, large black eyes, and hands of extraordinary delicacy. On being approached, they stepped backward into a patch of bracken and were gone, though the bracken was too thin to conceal a dog.

Their tools remained. They were made of a transparent substance lighter than wood and sharper than steel. The holes they had dug formed a pattern later recognised by a mathematician as part of a spiral diagram. Similar marks appeared in Richmond Park, Epping Forest, and the grounds of several cemeteries. The creatures, if creatures they were, avoided buildings but showed a marked interest in trees, clay, and graves.

The popular explanation was Martians. This satisfied the newspapers, who had grown lazy in their extraterrestrial vocabulary. Dr. Haldane proposed a subtler theory. The green men might not have crossed space at all, but scale. They could belong to a minute civilisation ordinarily below our notice, enlarged temporarily by some chemical or electrical process. Their terror of paved roads, horses, and boots supported this view.

The matter changed when a child found a green man injured beside a pond and fed him milk from a spoon. Before dying, the creature scratched a drawing on a slate: not of stars, but of a human foot descending upon a city of towers no higher than grass. The discovery produced, for a short time, a gentler manner of walking among the scientifically educated. It also opened the disquieting possibility that mankind had been barbarous toward nations it had never suspected beneath its shoes.
