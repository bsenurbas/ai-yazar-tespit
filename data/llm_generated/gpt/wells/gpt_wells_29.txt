The valley was found by surveyors correcting a railway line in Wales. Their chains shortened as they approached it. A mile measured by one instrument became three quarters by another, and a man walking across the field returned ten minutes before he had set out, pale and unwilling to speak.

Professor Lydgate concluded that space there had folded without tearing. Two distant points had come into an uneasy intimacy, like pages pressed together in a closed book. Stones thrown into the valley returned from behind the thrower. Sound crossed it in curves. A cottage at its centre appeared larger inside than out.

The railway company wished to avoid expense and bridge the anomaly. Lydgate opposed this with unusual passion. A bridge, he said, assumes that distance is honest. But in that valley, distance had become negotiable, and no empire built on timetables could long tolerate such an idea.
