The failure of mirrors began in hotels. A guest in Birmingham complained that his reflection lagged behind his movements by nearly a second. By evening, similar complaints reached manufacturers, barbers, actresses, and dentists across the country.

Physicists first suspected a defect in the silvering. Yet old mirrors, polished spoons, shop windows, and still water all showed the same delay. The reflected world had become slightly tardy. One could raise a hand, lower it, and then watch the image perform the gesture after one had already ceased.

The amusement ended when mirrors began to show movements that had not yet occurred. A man saw himself fall before he stumbled. A woman watched her reflection turn toward a door moments before anyone knocked. The mirror, that humble servant of vanity, had become the first crude instrument by which the future looked back.
