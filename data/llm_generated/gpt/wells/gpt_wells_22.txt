The insects came from no jungle and no ship’s cargo. They appeared in the glass cases of electrical shops, crawling over batteries and induction coils. Each was no larger than a grain of rice, with wings that shone blue in darkness and legs tipped like hooks of copper.

They fed on current. A wire carrying electricity drew them as sugar draws ants. At first they merely dimmed lamps and spoiled laboratory trials. Then they multiplied in power stations, telegraph exchanges, and tram depots, and whole districts found themselves returned to candlelight.

A naturalist observed that they built nests not of wax or fibre, but of magnetised filings. Within these nests the air hummed faintly, and the insects moved in patterns too exact for instinct. They were not pests in the old sense. They were the first citizens of a new ecology created by human invention.
