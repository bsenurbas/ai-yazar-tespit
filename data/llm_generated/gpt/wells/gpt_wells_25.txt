The polar expedition returned with one less man and one impossible map. The missing man, Havers, had walked ahead during a magnetic storm and vanished over level ice. Three days later his notebook was found, dry, warm, and written to the last page.

The entries described a valley beneath the aurora where the sky descended to the ground in curtains of green fire. Beyond the curtains stood cities, or things resembling cities, built of vertical light. Havers had drawn their outlines with a surveyor’s care and noted temperatures, bearings, and distances.

The map corresponded to no land on earth. Yet when its coordinates were compared with disturbances in wireless reception, they matched precisely a region above the pole, not upon it. Havers had not discovered a hidden country. He had stepped, briefly and fatally, into a structure suspended in the planet’s magnetic field.
