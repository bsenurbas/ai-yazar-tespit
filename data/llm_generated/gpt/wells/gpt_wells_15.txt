The alteration in sleep was first treated as a nervous fashion among students and clerks. Men slept for twelve hours and woke unrested; others slept for nine minutes and rose with the vigour of youth. Physicians blamed diet, gaslight, tobacco, heredity, and modern haste.

Dr. Vale noticed that the disorder followed longitude. At certain hours, an invisible line passed across the earth, and all who slept beneath it dreamed the same dream: a red plain, a cold wind, and towers moving slowly upon legs. The dream was reported by children, sailors, prisoners, and kings, none of whom had read the others’ accounts.

When the line reached London for the fourth time, Vale remained awake by force and watched the sleepers. Their eyes moved beneath closed lids in perfect unison. Their hands lifted slightly, as if reaching toward the same remote object. Mankind had thought sleep a private retreat; it was now becoming a channel.
