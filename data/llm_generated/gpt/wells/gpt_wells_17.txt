The island appeared between two scheduled passages of the mail steamer. There had been open water on Monday and land on Thursday: a low black shore, steaming in the morning light, with no birds about it and no surf upon its beach. The charts offered no assistance.

A landing party found the sand warm and springy, and the rocks so light that a man could carry a boulder under one arm. No plants grew there, but the surface was crossed by fine cracks that opened and closed with a rhythm unpleasantly like breathing. Captain Morley, a practical man, ordered the party back before noon.

As the boat pulled away, the island sank three feet and rose again. It was not volcanic. It was not coral. It was a floating organism of planetary scale, sleeping or feeding in the Atlantic, and the brief accident of its surfacing had introduced mankind to a form of life for which all our categories were provincial.
