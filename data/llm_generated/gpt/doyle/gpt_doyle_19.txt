The case of the folded map came to us from a young naval officer whose pride had not survived his panic. “A chart has vanished from my rooms,” said he, “and I am ruined unless it is found.” Holmes took from him a torn corner of blue paper. “Not vanished,” said he. “Copied. The original was returned badly.” “Returned?” “Yes. You have been too frightened to inspect your own drawer.”

The officer stared. Holmes continued: “The fold is wrong. A naval man folds a chart with the title outward; a civilian folds by convenience. There is cigar ash in the crease, and your rooms are pipe-smoked. The intruder was a guest, not a burglar.” “I entertained three men last night.” “Then we need only find the one who dislikes the sea but likes profit.”

By evening the three guests had been gathered under a pretext. Holmes laid the map upon the table and watched their hands. One man reached for it by the southern edge. Holmes smiled. “Thank you, Mr. Armitage. Only the copyist knows the tear lies there. You handled the chart last night, traced it, folded it wrongly, and placed it back with admirable nerve. Your ash did the rest.”
