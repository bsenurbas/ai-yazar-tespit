The adventure of the locked piano was among the more curious domestic puzzles of that year. A music teacher from Marylebone arrived with a key tied to a black ribbon. “My pupil has disappeared,” she said, “and the piano in her house is locked. It has never been locked before.” Holmes turned the key over. “Music is often less important than silence.”

At the house, the girl’s mother insisted there had been no quarrel. Holmes sat at the piano and tapped each key lightly. One gave no sound. “There is something beneath middle C,” he said. The mother gasped. “Impossible. The instrument was tuned yesterday.” Holmes looked at her. “By whom?” “A blind tuner.” “Then someone trusted his blindness and forgot his touch.”

Inside the piano lay a packet of letters and a railway timetable. “Your daughter has not been abducted,” Holmes said. “She fled from a marriage she did not want. The tuner carried her message out, and the locked piano delayed discovery.” He turned to the stepfather, whose anger had outrun his caution. “You locked it after finding the letters, but before removing the timetable. That was your error.”
