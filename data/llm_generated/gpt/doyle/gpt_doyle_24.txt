The adventure of the glass button occurred in a March gale which rattled the chimney-pots and drove half London indoors. A lady from Bayswater came to us with a button wrapped in tissue. “It was found in my husband’s study,” said she. “He has vanished, and I fear a woman is involved.” Holmes took the button and smiled. “A woman is involved, but not as you fear.”

The button was transparent glass, common enough upon ladies’ boots. Holmes held it to the firelight. “There is clay within the rim. Not street clay. Potter’s clay.” “My husband knows no potters.” “Then perhaps the potter knows your husband. The thread is black silk, but the garment was brown. A hurried replacement.” He glanced at me. “A disguise, Watson, and a clumsy one.”

In a small pottery near Fulham, Holmes found a ledger, a locked kiln room, and the missing husband alive but disgraced. “He came here to purchase forged antiquities,” Holmes said to the wife. “The woman whose button you found was the dealer’s assistant, dressed as a mourning widow to meet him unseen.” He tapped the glass button. “People look at faces. Buttons go about their business and tell the truth.”
