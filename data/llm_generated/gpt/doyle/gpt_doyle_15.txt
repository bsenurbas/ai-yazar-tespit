The affair of the missing mourning ring began upon a Sunday so still that even Baker Street appeared to hold its breath. A clergyman called upon us after luncheon, his manner gentle, his hands restless. “Mr. Holmes, a ring was taken from a coffin before burial.” Holmes’s eyes brightened. “From the finger?” “No. From a small box inside the coffin.” “That is better. A thief who opens a coffin for a box is either desperate or informed.”

The clergyman explained that the dead woman had left the ring to her estranged daughter, though no one knew where the daughter lived. Holmes listened without movement. “The box was forced?” “Yes.” “With a narrow blade?” “I believe so.” “Then the thief had no key and little time. Who wept most loudly?” “Her son.” “Ah. Loud grief is sometimes a curtain.”

At the chapel, Holmes found a smear of candle grease upon the coffin rail and a faint cut in the velvet lining. “The ring was not stolen after death,” said he. “It was removed before the coffin arrived and the box broken later to support a false story.” He turned toward the son, who had begun to breathe heavily. “You feared the daughter’s claim. Yet you forgot that wax drips downward. The candle mark proves the box was opened while it stood upright in your room, not lying in the coffin.”
