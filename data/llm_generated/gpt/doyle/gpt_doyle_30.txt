The adventure of the midnight umbrella began with a most respectable banker who arrived at Baker Street carrying an umbrella that was not his own. “It was left in my hall after a dinner party,” said he. “This morning my strongbox was open.” Holmes looked at the umbrella and laughed softly. “My dear sir, the umbrella did not come after the dinner. It came before it.”

The banker seemed offended. Holmes continued: “The silk is dry, though last night was wet. The ferrule bears fresh mud from your garden, not the street. Someone placed it in the hall from inside the house, then used it as a marker.” “A marker?” “Yes. To tell an accomplice which coat peg concealed the strongbox key.” He glanced at me. “A domestic crime, Watson. The umbrella is only the signpost.”

At the banker’s house, Holmes examined the hall stand and found a tiny scratch upon the third peg. “Here,” said he. “The key was hung under a cloak. The umbrella guided the hand in darkness.” The butler denied all knowledge until Holmes opened the umbrella and shook out a folded betting slip. “Your accomplice was careless with his amusements. A man may hide a key, but he should never hide his habits beside it.”
