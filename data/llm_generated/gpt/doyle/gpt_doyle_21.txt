The affair of the green candle was brought by a housekeeper from Bloomsbury, a capable woman who spoke as though every word had been scrubbed before use. “My master died during supper,” she said. “The doctor says apoplexy. I say murder.” Holmes gave her a keen look. “You are loyal.” “I am observant.” “A better quality for our purpose.”

Her evidence was a half-burned green candle from the dining table. Holmes scraped it with his knife. “Not wax alone,” said he. “There is resin in it, and something else.” I bent close. “A scent?” “Laudanum, faint but present. The candle was not meant to kill. It was meant to stupefy.” The housekeeper folded her hands. “Then someone wished him helpless before he died.”

At the house, Holmes discovered a draft from the window and a chair placed slightly too far from the table. “The victim was drugged by vapour, then moved,” said he. “The candle burned beside him while the murderer searched the desk.” He turned to the niece, who had claimed illness upstairs. “You chose green wax because it matched the table setting. Taste, madam, is no shield against chemistry.”
