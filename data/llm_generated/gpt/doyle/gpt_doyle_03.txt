It was in the winter of ’89, during one of those yellow London afternoons when the fog appears not to descend from the heavens but to rise, like some unwholesome vapour, from the very stones. Holmes had been conducting a chemical experiment of so pungent a character that I had twice opened the window and twice been ordered to close it, when our sitting-room door flew open and Inspector Lestrade stepped briskly in. “A queer one, Mr. Holmes,” said he. “A jeweller in Holborn has been robbed, yet his safe is untouched.” Holmes laid down his glass tube. “Then the safe was not the object.” “But the diamonds are gone.” “Ah,” said Holmes, “then the diamonds were not in the safe.”

Lestrade frowned, while I confess I could make little of my friend’s remark. “The man swears they were,” said the inspector. “A tray of unset stones, valued at six thousand pounds.” Holmes smiled thinly. “And he swore it after consulting his ledger, no doubt. Watson, note the distinction between memory and bookkeeping.” He turned to Lestrade. “Was there flour upon the floor?” “Flour?” “Or fine dust. Powder. Anything pale.” “There was plaster near the counter.” Holmes clapped his hands softly. “Good. Was the jeweller’s assistant newly engaged, left-handed, and troubled by a cough?” Lestrade stared. “He is left-handed, yes. How the devil—” “By the burglary, Inspector. A right-handed thief opens a drawer from the front. A left-handed man, working in haste from behind the counter, scores the inner edge. The cough explains why he chose plaster dust: it masks the scent of the tobacco he dropped.”

By seven o’clock we stood in the narrow Holborn shop, where Holmes bent over the counter with the grave attention of a scholar before a manuscript. “The solution is almost vulgar,” said he at last. “The diamonds were removed before the alarm was raised. The safe was left untouched to suggest an external thief. The plaster was shaken out after the event to imply entry through the ceiling, yet the ceiling bears no true break, only theatrical damage.” He turned to the trembling jeweller. “You dismissed your assistant yesterday, but kept his spare key to accuse him today. Unfortunately, you forgot that plaster falls downward, not upward. The dust upon the underside of the shelf condemns you. Lestrade, your thief is not outside in the fog. He is behind his own counter.”
