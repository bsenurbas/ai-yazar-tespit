The matter of the bent hatpin came to us through Inspector Gregson, who disliked asking Holmes for aid almost as much as he disliked being wrong without him. “A lady has been attacked in Hyde Park,” said he. “She names no assailant.” Holmes accepted the hatpin. “A lady who names no assailant may be protecting one or accusing none.”

The pin was long, steel, and bent near the tip. Holmes tested it against his thumb. “Bent against bone? No. Against metal. She struck a watch chain or locket.” Gregson looked doubtful. “There was no locket.” “Then it was removed. Did she wear gloves?” “Yes.” “Were they torn?” “One was.” Holmes nodded. “She defended herself, but not from a stranger. A stranger attacks the purse. An acquaintance attacks the letter.”

The answer lay in a small photograph shop near the park, where Holmes found a smashed frame and a missing negative. “The lady was not attacked for money,” he said. “She fought to keep a photograph from being taken.” He faced a young officer whose watch chain bore a fresh scratch. “The hatpin met your chain, sir. You may deny the park, but your waistcoat has already placed you there.”
