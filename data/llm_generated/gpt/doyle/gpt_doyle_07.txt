The adventure of the blackened visiting card was, in its first appearance, so slight a matter that I should not have recorded it had it not led us into one of the strangest domestic conspiracies of that season. It was a cold afternoon, with a brown fog pressing against the windows and the muffled roar of London coming faintly from the street below. Holmes had been sorting a heap of correspondence with his usual disdain when he paused over a card scorched at one corner. “Watson,” said he, “here is a man who has lost a fortune, found a secret, and come to me too late.” At that moment the bell rang, and our visitor was shown in: a thin, elegant gentleman whose gloves were new, though his coat was brushed threadbare at the seams.

“My name is Charles Harbury,” said he, “and I beg your help in recovering a missing will.” Holmes motioned him to continue, but his eyes never left the card. “It was in my uncle’s desk. Last night there was a small fire in the study. Nothing was destroyed save a few papers, yet this morning the will was gone.” Holmes held up the card. “You found this upon the hearth?” “Yes.” “And assumed it belonged to the intruder?” “Naturally.” “A poor assumption. The card was burned before it was placed there. See the clean ash at the edge, while the study fire, I think, burned coal. This corner was held to a candle. Someone wished you to believe in hurried destruction, not careful selection.”

By evening we were in the uncle’s study, where Holmes examined the desk, the grate, the bell-pull, and finally a row of books beside the window. “There was no burglary,” said he. “The thief had leisure, knowledge, and a key to this room. The will was not burned; it was replaced.” He drew out a volume of sermons and opened it to reveal a folded parchment hidden inside the hollowed cover. Our client gave a cry, but Holmes raised a hand. “Not so fast, Mr. Harbury. This is the old will. The new one, which disinherits you, is in your pocket, where you placed it after taking it from the desk. You came to me not to recover a document, but to learn whether your trick had been detected. It has.”
