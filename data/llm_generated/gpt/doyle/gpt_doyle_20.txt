The adventure of the porcelain dog began with a child, which was unusual enough to put Holmes in excellent humour. The boy, no more than twelve, stood in our room with wet boots and a brave chin. “My mother says Father ran away,” he said. “But Father would not leave Toby.” He placed a cracked porcelain dog upon Holmes’s table.

Holmes softened in that quiet way he rarely allowed others to see. “And who is Toby?” “Our real dog, sir. This was on Father’s desk.” Holmes examined the ornament. “Your father was a clerk, careful with money, and left home before breakfast.” The boy nodded. “This dog is cracked at the base, yet the crack contains fresh ink. It was used as a paperweight after being broken. Your father left a message beneath it.”

We found the message in the base, a strip of paper rolled tight. It led us to a warehouse near the river, where the father had been held by men whose false accounts he had uncovered. “Your father did not run away,” Holmes told the boy afterward. “He hid the truth in the one object no adult thought worth touching.” He glanced at me as the child left with tears in his eyes. “Watson, children are neglected witnesses because they notice without being noticed.”
