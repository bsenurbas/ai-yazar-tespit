The adventure of the torn railway ticket began at breakfast, when Holmes tossed the morning paper aside with impatience. “A banker robbed on the Brighton line,” said he. “The police are hunting a stranger with a beard.” “And you?” I asked. “I am hunting a man without one.”

The banker arrived an hour later, shaken and indignant. “My wallet was taken while I slept.” Holmes held out his hand. “Your ticket.” The gentleman produced half of it. Holmes studied the tear. “You were not asleep when the wallet disappeared. You tore this yourself while struggling to keep your account of the journey intact.” The banker flushed. “Sir!” “Come now. Your cuff bears coffee, not chloroform.”

At Victoria Station, Holmes questioned the guard and found the missing half of the ticket in a waste bin near the refreshment room. “The theft was staged,” he said. “Our banker lost the money earlier and invented a railway villain.” He turned to the man with cold amusement. “The beard was a mercy to your imagination, but not to the facts. A torn ticket, like a torn conscience, has two halves.”
