The matter of the cracked bell-rope was brought to us on a rain-lashed evening, when the windows of our Baker Street rooms rattled under a bitter east wind. Holmes sat by the fire, his violin untouched upon his knee, while I read aloud some trifling account of a burglary in Kensington. “A burglary without theft is rarely burglary,” said he, just as Mrs. Hudson entered with a card. “Miss Eleanor Vane,” he read. “Show her in.” Our visitor was a pale young lady in mourning, whose composure failed the instant she crossed the threshold. “Mr. Holmes, my uncle died last night, and the police call it natural. I know it was not.” Holmes motioned her to the chair. “Then we shall begin with what you know, and end, perhaps, with what we can prove.”

“My uncle was found in his study,” said she, “the door locked within, the window fastened, and the bell-rope torn from the wall.” Holmes leaned forward. “Torn, you say, not cut?” “Yes, sir.” “Excellent. A cut rope speaks of design; a torn one, of struggle or haste. Was the fire burning?” “Low, but not out.” “And the lamp?” “Turned down.” Holmes glanced at me. “You observe, Watson, a man who expects no visitor lowers a lamp; a man who fears discovery extinguishes it. Pray continue, Miss Vane.” She clasped her gloves tightly. “There was also a smell of bitter almonds.” Holmes’s eyes narrowed. “That, my dear lady, is the first fact in your story which may be a false one.”

Within the hour we were in the dead man’s study, where Holmes moved with his customary silence from hearth to desk, from bell-pull to carpet. At last he pointed to three small scratches upon the brass fender. “The bell-rope was not pulled by your uncle,” said he. “It was used to lower something from the chimney. The almond scent was placed afterward to mislead a provincial doctor toward poison. Your uncle died of fright, Miss Vane, but the fright had a human author.” He turned sharply toward the butler, who had grown ashen by the door. “You may save us some trouble by telling Inspector Lestrade where you hid the iron mask.”
