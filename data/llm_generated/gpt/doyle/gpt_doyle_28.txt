The adventure of the brown envelope began with a schoolmaster whose dignity had been badly shaken. “Examination papers have been stolen,” said he. “The honour of the college is at stake.” Holmes smiled faintly. “Honour is often most visible when threatened by ink.”

The envelope bore no address, only a smear of flour and a pinhole near the seal. Holmes examined it with care. “It was opened by steam, then pinned shut while drying. The flour suggests a baker’s boy, but the fold suggests a scholar. Has any pupil access to the kitchen?” “Several.” “Then we shall not begin with pupils. We shall begin with appetite.”

At the college, Holmes inspected the dining hall and the study where the papers had been kept. “The thief entered during supper,” he said. “Not to take the papers, but to read them through the envelope. The pinhole allowed a wire to lift the edge.” He turned to the assistant master. “Your mistake was flour. No boy would leave it on the envelope; a grown man using a false clue would apply too much.”
