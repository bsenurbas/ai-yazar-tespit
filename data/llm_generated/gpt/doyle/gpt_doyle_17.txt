The matter of the ivory bookmark began with a bookseller from Charing Cross, who arrived with spectacles askew and terror in every line of his face. “A gentleman was murdered after buying a volume from my shop,” he cried. “They say I sold him the weapon.” Holmes raised an eyebrow. “Books may wound, but seldom in the police sense.”

The weapon, it appeared, was a slender ivory bookmark sharpened to a point. Holmes examined it under the lamp. “This was not sharpened by a murderer,” said he. “It was trimmed by a restorer. See the even scrape, Watson. The point is accidental. The true weapon lies elsewhere.” “But it was found beside the body,” said the bookseller. “Objects beside bodies are often put there by the living.”

In the dead man’s library, Holmes opened the purchased book and found a square of paper hidden within the spine. “A code,” said I. “A list,” Holmes replied. “Names and payments.” He looked toward the nephew, who had claimed to find the body. “You placed the bookmark to accuse the bookseller, but you overlooked the glue. Fresh binding paste under your thumbnail tells us who opened the spine before the police arrived.”
