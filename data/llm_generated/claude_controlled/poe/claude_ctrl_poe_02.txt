There are towns which seem to exist at the sufferance of the landscape rather than by any assertion of human will, and ours was such a town; it clung to the valley floor with the tenacity of something that has been told it does not belong and has chosen, for reasons known only to itself, to remain regardless. We were not prosperous. We were not, in any sense that a traveler passing through might have recognized, particularly alive. We existed, which is to say we endured, which is to say we had arranged ourselves into the habits and routines of endurance so thoroughly that we had long since ceased to distinguish between living and the performance of living.

It was into this arrangement that the stranger descended, on a night of such stillness that the sound of his footsteps on the road reached me before I could see him, and I heard him—that measured, unhurried percussion against the packed earth—for what seemed an extraordinary length of time before his figure resolved itself from the darkness at the edge of my vision. He was tall, or appeared so; the night has a way of adding to a man's dimensions when there is nothing nearby to provide a corrective scale. He carried nothing that I could discern. He wore a coat of some dark material that absorbed the moonlight rather than reflecting it, so that he seemed, as he approached, to be less a solid presence moving through the night than a displacement of it—a place where the darkness had organized itself into a shape and was now moving with purpose toward the center of our small and inadequate light.

I am not, by nature or by habit, a man given to premonitions. I have lived my life, such as it has been, in the close company of the ordinary, and the ordinary has generally been sufficient. But as I watched him cross the threshold of our town, something in me—some faculty older and less rational than reason—registered his arrival not as the event it appeared to be but as the consequence of some prior event, long ago and far away, whose effects had been traveling toward us ever since, gathering weight and direction as they came, until they had taken the form of this man, on this night, walking toward us with the patience of the inevitable.
