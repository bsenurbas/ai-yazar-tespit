The inn at the center of our town was not, by any standard that a traveler accustomed to the comforts of more prosperous places might apply, a welcoming establishment; it was old, and it wore its age not with the dignity of things that have been well maintained but with the resignation of things that have outlasted the care that was once taken of them. The sign above the door moved in the wind with a sound that I had long since ceased to hear as a sound and had come to accept as simply a property of the night, as natural and unremarkable as the wind itself. It was this sign that the stranger looked at first—before the door, before the windows, before any of the faces that might have been visible within—and he looked at it with an attention so concentrated, so specific, so oddly personal, that I had the impression he had been told about this sign, had formed an expectation of it, and was now measuring the reality against the expectation and finding the correspondence exact.

Everything about him suggested this quality of confirmation rather than discovery. He had not come to find; he had come to verify. The distinction seems small, but it is not; it is, I have since concluded, the whole of the difference between a visitor and something else—something for which our language of hospitality and travel does not have a comfortable name. A visitor is a person to whom one's home is new; what stood before our inn that night was a person to whom our home was, in some sense and by some means I could not account for, already known.

He entered without knocking—or so it seemed to me; I may have missed the knock, distracted as I was by the intensity of my own observation—and the door closed behind him with a finality that was out of proportion to the quietness of its closing; it closed the way conclusions close, the way things close that are not expected to open again in the same configuration. And then there was silence; and the silence had a different quality than the silence that had preceded his arrival, as all silences do that follow upon the entry of the inevitable into the realm of the actual.
