Let me be precise about what I observed, for precision is the only defense I have against the accusation—which I level at myself before anyone else can level it—that what I experienced was the product of a mind already disposed toward the extraordinary and therefore inclined to find it where it does not exist. I was at my window. The night was clear. The moon was sufficient. The stranger was visible from the moment he turned the corner at the far end of our street to the moment he reached the inn door, a distance I have since measured at approximately ninety yards. I watched him for the entirety of this distance. I was not drowsy. I had not been drinking. My eyesight is, and was at that time, excellent. These are the conditions of my observation; I record them so that the observation itself may be evaluated against them.

What I observed was a man walking. He was of above average height, dressed in dark clothing, moving at a pace that I can only describe as deliberate—not slow, but without the quality of ordinary movement that suggests a person unconsciously navigating space, making the small adjustments and accommodations of a consciousness only partly engaged with its physical surroundings. He was entirely engaged with his physical surroundings. He moved through our street as though each step were a decision, considered and confirmed, rather than an automatic consequence of the previous step. This gave his approach a quality that I found, and continue to find, deeply unsettling, though I confess that my unsettlement may say more about my own state of mind than about anything objectively present in what I saw.

And yet—I return to this, as I have returned to it many times in the months since—there was the matter of the shadows. The shadows fell, as shadows should, away from the light source: away from the moon, away from the lamp above the inn door. The stranger's shadow fell, as I watched, in the correct direction. But it fell slightly behind him—not behind him as a shadow falls behind a man moving toward a light source, which would be explicable, but behind him as a shadow falls behind a man who is moving faster than his shadow has yet been informed.
