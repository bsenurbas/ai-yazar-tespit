The night our visitor arrived was the night the last of the autumn leaves fell from the oak at the center of the square—a coincidence which I record not because I attach to it any significance beyond what it possesses as a fact, but because I have found, in the months since, that I am unable to think of one without thinking of the other; they have become, in my memory, a single event, the falling of the leaves and the appearance of the man, as though the tree had been waiting for his arrival to complete its seasonal surrender and had timed its final release to coincide with the first sound of his footsteps on our road.

He came late, when the town had already drawn its shutters and settled into the particular quiet that distinguishes our nights from our days not merely by the absence of activity but by a quality of attention that the darkness brings with it—a sense that the town is listening for something it cannot name and has been listening for longer than anyone currently living can remember. Into this listening he arrived, and I will say only that the quality of the silence changed when he came; it became, if such a thing is possible, more attentive, as though whatever the town had been listening for had finally, after its long patience, begun to make itself heard.

I saw him from my window—I keep late hours, a habit of solitude that has served me in ways I did not anticipate—and I watched him traverse our single street with the slow deliberation of a man who is memorizing what he sees. He did not look about him with the curiosity of a traveler encountering the unfamiliar; he looked about him with the recognition of a man returning to a place he has known in some form other than its present one, verifying the correspondence between the version in his memory and the version before his eyes. When he raised his face toward my window—and I am certain, though I had given him no reason to suspect my presence there, that he knew I was watching—his expression was one I had seen before only in mirrors, at moments of extreme and private extremity: the expression of a man who has finally arrived at the place his whole life has been moving toward, and is not certain whether to call that arrival a destination or a verdict.
