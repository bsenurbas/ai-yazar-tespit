What is it that we recognize, in the arrival of certain strangers, before we have any rational basis for recognition? I put this question not rhetorically but with the genuine urgency of a man who has spent many months attempting to answer it and has found, at each attempt, that the question recedes before him like a corridor whose end is always visible and never reached. For I recognized him—I am compelled to use that word, inadequate as it is—the moment he appeared at the far end of our street, though I had never seen his face, never heard his name, never been given any description that would have allowed me to identify him in advance. I recognized him the way one recognizes the fulfillment of a dread one has carried so long that the dread itself has become a kind of companion; with something that was not quite relief and not quite terror but partook of both.

He moved through our town with the careful attention of a man reading a text he has been long anticipating. Each building seemed to occupy his gaze for a moment—not the idle gaze of curiosity, but the concentrated gaze of verification, as though he were confirming the correspondence between what he saw and what he had been told to expect. The inn, the church, the well at the center of the square: each received its moment of scrutiny and was apparently found satisfactory, for he continued, unhurried, toward the inn's door, where the lamp cast its small and faltering democracy of light upon the mud of the threshold.

I had withdrawn from the window by the time he knocked—withdrawn not from any conscious decision but from the instinctive recoil of a man who realizes, too late, that he has been watching something he was not meant to see; or rather, something that knew it was being watched and had permitted it, for reasons of its own. The knock, when it came, was neither loud nor tentative. It was precisely as loud as a knock needs to be to be heard by those who are waiting for it, and no louder. And we were all, I understood at that moment, waiting for it. We had been waiting for it, in one way or another, for a very long time.
