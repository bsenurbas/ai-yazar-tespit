The stranger arrived at half past eleven, which was itself informative. The last coach from the junction reached our town at nine; the first of the morning left at six. A man arriving on foot at half past eleven had either missed the coach deliberately or had come from somewhere the coach did not go. Given the state of his boots—which I observed as he crossed the threshold of the inn, before he had removed his coat or spoken to anyone—the latter seemed more probable. The boots were good quality but showed the particular pattern of wear that results from rough terrain rather than road walking: the outer edge of each heel was worn more than the inner, which is characteristic of a man who picks his way across uneven ground rather than following a maintained surface.

"You'll want a room," said the innkeeper, which was not a question but a statement of the obvious, and the stranger confirmed it with a nod that managed to convey both assent and a preference for brevity. He was perhaps forty, lean, with the kind of face that has been weathered by outdoor life rather than by age, and his hands, when he reached into his coat for his wallet, were the hands of a man who works with them. Not a professional man, then—or not primarily. Someone who had been a professional man once, perhaps, and had become something else.

I was there that evening in my capacity as the town's medical officer, attending a minor matter that had brought me to the inn rather than to my surgery, and I had no reason to concern myself with a stranger whose arrival, however late, was not in itself remarkable. But there was something in the quality of his attention—the way he scanned the room in the moment before he appeared to be scanning it, the way his eyes catalogued exits and occupants with the efficiency of long habit—that suggested a man for whom vigilance was not a response to a specific threat but a general condition of existence.

"Traveling far?" the innkeeper asked, with the genial intrusiveness of his profession.

"Far enough," the stranger said, and the way he said it closed the subject with a finality that the innkeeper, to his credit, recognized and respected.
