The arrival of a stranger in a small town is always, to the observant eye, a source of information disproportionate to the brevity of the encounter. People in transit reveal themselves more completely than people at rest, because the usual defenses of social familiarity have not yet been assembled; one sees them, for a moment, as they are rather than as they have arranged to be seen. The man who entered the Crown that evening at approximately eleven o'clock was no exception to this principle, and I found myself, as I often do in such circumstances, cataloguing his characteristics before I had made any conscious decision to do so.

Age: forty-two or forty-three. The face was younger than the hands, which is common in men who have spent time in climates that are hard on skin. Occupation: something that required both precision and physical endurance—the fine motor control visible in the way he handled his coat buttons was inconsistent with purely manual labor, while the broader musculature of his upper body was inconsistent with purely desk work. There was a small scar above his left eyebrow, old and well healed, that had been made by something with a straight edge. Not a fight; the angle was wrong. An accident, perhaps, of the kind that happens in workshops or laboratories.

He ordered ale rather than spirits, which suggested a man who wanted to be clear-headed rather than a man who wanted to stop thinking. He drank slowly, and while he drank he was still in the way that men are still when they are thinking rather than resting—the stillness of active concentration, not of relaxation. Twice he reached into his coat pocket and then appeared to change his mind about whatever he had been going to remove from it. A letter, most likely, that he had read many times and did not need to read again but kept reaching for from force of habit.

He was waiting to make a decision, and the decision was not a comfortable one.
