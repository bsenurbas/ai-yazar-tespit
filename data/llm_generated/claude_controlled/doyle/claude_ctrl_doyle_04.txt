"Tell me what you make of him," said Farrell, as the stranger settled himself at a table near the window and accepted the menu from the innkeeper's wife with the brief courtesy of a man who has more on his mind than food.

I studied the figure for a moment. "He's been traveling for some time—not today only, but for several days at least. The fatigue in his shoulders isn't the fatigue of an afternoon's journey; it goes deeper than that, into the muscles that support sustained effort. His coat is damp at the collar from last night's rain, which means he slept in it, or at least spent part of the night outdoors. The right cuff shows ink stains that are at least two days old, which suggests he was writing under conditions that didn't allow for proper cleanup. He's not a vagrant—the coat is too good, and there's a quality to his posture even now, tired as he is, that speaks to someone accustomed to presenting himself in a certain way. Professional of some kind. Recently displaced from whatever situation that profession normally keeps him in."

"Anything else?"

"He's looking for someone. Not watching the door in the way of a man who fears being followed—he's too calm for that—but watching it in the way of a man who expects a specific person to walk through it. He checks it every few minutes, not compulsively but methodically, the way one checks a clock when waiting for an appointment." I paused. "And he's been here before. He knew where to sit without looking around for the best vantage point. He walked directly to that table."

Farrell nodded slowly. "I thought as much. The question is when, and under what circumstances."

"And whether the person he's waiting for knows he's here," I added. "Those are rather different situations, with rather different implications."
