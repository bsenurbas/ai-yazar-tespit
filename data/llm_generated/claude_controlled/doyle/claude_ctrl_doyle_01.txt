It was Merrick who first noticed the stranger, and it was characteristic of the man that his observation was both immediate and precise. "He came in on the north road," he said, setting down his glass, "sometime after ten. Walked the whole way from Ashford, I'd say, though he's taken some trouble to make it look otherwise."

I had not seen the man enter the inn, having been absorbed in the letter I was composing, but I looked up now and found him easily enough: a lean figure at the far end of the bar, unremarkable in dress, ordering what appeared to be a glass of water with the air of a man who requires sustenance of no other kind.

"What makes you say Ashford?" I asked.

"The clay on his left boot is from the Ashford road—there's a distinctive red streak through it that you don't find anywhere else within twenty miles. He's tried to clean it off, which is interesting in itself. And the sole wear on the right boot is consistent with someone who favors the grass verge when walking, which you do on the north road because the surface is rough. He's been walking for at least four hours." Merrick picked up his glass again. "The question that interests me is why a man who clearly doesn't want to be identified as having come from Ashford is staying in the only inn in a town where everyone will eventually know exactly where he came from."

It was, I reflected, exactly the right question. The stranger had chosen his position at the bar with care—his back was not quite to the room, but he was angled away from the majority of its occupants, and he was watching the door in the bar mirror with the practiced casualness of a man who has learned that watching a door directly invites attention. He was expecting someone, or fearing someone, or both.

"We should speak to him," I said.

"We will," said Merrick, with the patient certainty that I had come to recognize as his habitual mode when he had already determined the shape of what was to come. "But not yet. Let him finish his water. A man who has walked four hours in the cold has earned the right to collect himself before we take away whatever composure he has managed to assemble."
