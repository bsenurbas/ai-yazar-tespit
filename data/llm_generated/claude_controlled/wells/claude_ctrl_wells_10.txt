I have tried, in writing this account, to be precise about what I actually observed as opposed to what I subsequently inferred, because the two categories have a way of merging in memory in a manner that is not always obvious to the person doing the remembering. We observe less than we think we observe, and we infer more than we realize we infer, and the result is a version of events that has the feel of direct experience but the structure of narrative reconstruction. I cannot guarantee that everything I have described as observation was not, in some cases, inference; I can only say that I have tried to keep them separate.

What I observed, then, was this: a man of ordinary appearance engaging in systematic and methodical behavior that was not fully explained by the professional identity he offered. The behavior was consistent across eleven days and across multiple contexts—the inn, the streets, the conversations he had with various residents—suggesting that it was not incidental or recreational but purposeful. The purpose was not disclosed.

What I inferred was that the purpose related to the physical characteristics of the town—its geography, its geology, its infrastructure—rather than to its human population, since his engagement with people seemed to be primarily instrumental, directed at extracting specific kinds of information rather than at establishing relationships or gathering the more complex social intelligence that would have been necessary for other kinds of purposes.

What has since become clear—and here I move from observation and inference into the territory of knowledge acquired after the fact—is that both my observations and my inferences were correct as far as they went, and that they did not go nearly far enough. The town was being assessed, and the assessment was methodical, and the purpose was connected to the physical characteristics of the place. All of that was right. What was wrong was the assumption, which I made without realizing I was making it, that the entity conducting the assessment was human.
