There is a tendency, which I have observed in myself as much as in others, to treat the unfamiliar as though it were simply the familiar in disguise—to read the new through the templates of the known, because the known is comfortable and the new is not. This tendency served us badly in the matter of the stranger, and it is worth examining why, not because the examination will change what happened, but because similar situations will arise and the same mistake will be made unless there is some record of how it was made this time.

He came to us on a night of light rain, which had the effect of making his arrival seem more natural than it was—people arriving in rain attract less scrutiny than people arriving in clear weather, because the rain provides an explanation for their condition and a reason for their haste. He was not, as it happened, in any haste, but the rain covered this as well, since a man who walks at a measured pace in the rain can be taken for a man who has simply given up on keeping dry.

He asked the right questions at the inn—room, meal, hours—and he answered the innkeeper's conversational inquiries with the brief, uninformative responses of a man who is tired rather than a man who is evasive. These two things look identical from the outside, and we chose, as people generally do, the more comfortable interpretation.

In the morning he was methodical. He rose early, ate breakfast, and went out with the notebook. He spent approximately forty minutes walking the streets in a pattern that, I later realized, was not random but systematic—a grid, overlaid on the irregular geography of the town, walked in sections that he checked off as he completed them. At the time I thought he was familiarizing himself with the layout. He was, but not in the way I assumed. He was looking for something specific, and he was looking for it in the way of a man who has a fairly good idea of where it will be found and is working through the possibilities in order.

He found it on the second day. What happened after that is a different kind of story.
