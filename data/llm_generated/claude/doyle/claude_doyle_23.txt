"You've been followed," said Holmes, as I entered Baker Street and closed the door behind me.

I turned in some surprise. "I noticed nothing."

"No. You were not meant to. The man is good—better than the usual sort Moriarty employs, which suggests this is not a routine surveillance but something more purposeful." Holmes moved to the window and looked down at the street with the careful inattention of a man who does not wish to be seen looking. "He picked you up at the Strand and followed you to within twenty yards of this door, at which point he turned off. He knows where you were going and saw no need to confirm it."

"What does he want?"

"Not you specifically, I think. You are a means to an end, as you have occasionally been before, and I apologize for it, though I confess it is sometimes useful to have a friend whose movements are less routinely observed than my own." He turned from the window. "The question is what end. The man who followed you is not a footpad or a common spy. He is a professional of some experience, and professionals of experience are not deployed for minor purposes." He crossed to his desk and picked up a letter that lay there. "This arrived this morning. Read it."

I read it. It was brief: a single line, unsigned, which said that Holmes was in possession of something that did not belong to him, and that arrangements would be made to recover it.

"What do you possess that someone wants?" I asked.

"That," said Holmes, with the particular brightness in his eyes that meant the game had genuinely engaged him, "is an excellent question. Because I have been through everything in this room, and I cannot identify it. Which means either that I have something I don't know I have, or that someone believes I have something I don't. Both possibilities are interesting."
