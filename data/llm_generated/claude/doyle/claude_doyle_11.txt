"You've been to the docks this morning," said Holmes, without looking up from the chemical apparatus that had occupied him since before I arrived. "Wapping, specifically, and you spoke with a harbour official rather than a merchant, which suggests you were seeking information rather than conducting business of your own."

The young man in the doorway stared. He was perhaps twenty-five, well-dressed but with the look of someone whose clothes had been chosen by a person more accustomed to comfort than to fashion. "I did go to the docks," he said. "How could you possibly—"

"The mud on your right boot is river mud from the lower Thames, the clay composition of which differs from the upper reaches. You have a smear of red oxide on your left cuff, the type used to treat the ironwork on dock cranes and bollards, not on merchant vessels. And you have been standing at a counter or a high desk for some time—the posture you carry is the posture of a man who has been leaning forward to speak quietly to someone across a high surface." Holmes set down his pipette and turned at last. "Come in, Mr. Hartwell. I have been expecting someone on your errand, though I confess I had anticipated your brother rather than yourself. Sit down and tell me what they found in the cargo manifest that they were not supposed to find."

The young man sat. The colour had not returned to his face. "My brother said you were—that is, he said you sometimes seemed to know things before they had been told to you."

"Your brother is kind. I know nothing before it is told to me. I simply listen to rather more of what is being told than most people consider worth attending to." Holmes pressed his fingertips together. "The manifest. If you please."
