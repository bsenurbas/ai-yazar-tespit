"The difficulty," Holmes said, setting down the report and rubbing his eyes with the gesture that meant he had been reading for too long and had found too little, "is that everyone in this case is telling the truth. That is far more inconvenient than the alternative."

I looked up from my own reading. "Everyone?"

"As far as I can determine. The butler saw what he says he saw. The housemaid heard what she says she heard. The guest who claims to have been in the library all evening was, in fact, in the library all evening—I have verified this independently. And the host, who says he has no idea how a dead man came to be in his locked study, genuinely has no idea." Holmes stood and went to the window. "A crime that is witnessed accurately by multiple honest people and that is nevertheless inexplicable is either a very clever trick or a failure of my own reasoning. I try to be honest about which is more probable."

"Which do you think it is?"

"I think I am missing something that is directly in front of me, which is the most annoying category of oversight." He turned. "Go back to the beginning. Not the beginning of the evening—the beginning of the acquaintance. How did the host know the dead man? Not what he told us. What we can verify." He reached for his coat. "We have been examining the scene of the death. We have not examined the scene of whatever happened between these two men that made the death, however it was accomplished, feel to someone like a necessity."
