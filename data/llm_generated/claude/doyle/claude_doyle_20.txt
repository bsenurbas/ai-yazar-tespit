Holmes's ability to reconstruct the recent past from physical evidence was, as I have recorded elsewhere, remarkable; but what was perhaps less remarked upon in my accounts was his equal ability to predict the immediate future from the same evidence, an ability that derived not from any supernatural faculty but from the simple and disciplined habit of following a chain of reasoning to its necessary conclusion rather than stopping at the point where it became uncomfortable.

It was this habit that caused him, upon our arrival at the Kenmore house, to instruct the constable on duty to send immediately for a locksmith, before we had entered the building or spoken to anyone within it.

"The front door has been relocked from the outside since the body was discovered," Holmes explained, when the constable looked at him with understandable confusion. "The scratches around the keyhole are fresh—made within the last six hours—and they are on the exterior of the lock, not the interior. Which means someone has returned to this house since the discovery was reported, has found it guarded, and has not been able to gain entry by the front door." He looked at the constable steadily. "Which means they will try another way. The locksmith is not to open anything. He is to tell me which lock, if any, has been interfered with since yesterday morning. That will tell us where our visitor tried, and failing the front door, which room they considered worth the attempt."

He turned to me. "Watson, the garden, if you please. Specifically any ground beneath a window that has been recently disturbed. Take your time—we are looking for someone who is still in the vicinity and knows we are here."
