My cousin, who was the last of our line to inhabit the ancestral estate before its sale, left behind a journal of some four hundred pages, of which the first three hundred and ninety concern themselves with the ordinary administration of a declining property: tenant disputes, drainage failures, the slow deterioration of the north wing's roof. It was only in the final ten pages that the tone changed, and changed so abruptly, so completely, that I have sometimes wondered whether the later entries were written by the same hand, or whether the same hand had, by the time it wrote them, ceased in any meaningful sense to belong to the same person.

The final entry is dated—and here I must pause, for the date is the source of the difficulty that has occupied me these past months—three days after the date that appears in the parish record as that of my cousin's death. I have verified both dates independently. I have consulted two archivists and a handwriting expert, all of whom confirm what I already knew: the handwriting is my cousin's, the date in the journal is later than the date on the stone, and there is no rational explanation that does not require one to discard either the evidence of documents or the evidence of graves.

The entry itself is brief. It reads: "The room at the end of the east corridor is not empty, as I previously recorded. I was mistaken about the emptiness. I was also mistaken about the door, about the lock, and about the nature of what I believed I had secured within. I leave this correction for whoever comes after, with the sincere hope that they will be wiser than I was, and with the less sincere hope—for hope, by the time I write this, has become a word I use from habit rather than conviction—that the correction will reach them in time to be of use."
