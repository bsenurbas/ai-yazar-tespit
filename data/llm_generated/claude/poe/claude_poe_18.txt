The last entry in my predecessor's journal was dated the fourteenth of November and read, in its entirety: it is not the darkness I object to. It is that the darkness has learned my name. Below this, in a different ink and what appeared to be a considerably less steady hand, was a single additional line that had been added at some later time: and now it uses it. The journal contained no further entries. My predecessor had vacated the position of night watchman at the Aldermere Archive three days after the fourteenth of November, and had not, to the knowledge of the Archive's administrators, been in contact with anyone associated with the institution since.

I was his replacement. I had been given this information in the manner of information that is provided because concealing it entirely would constitute a legal liability, but whose significance is minimized through tone and context until it seems not quite alarming enough to act upon. The Archive's director had described my predecessor as having left for personal reasons, and had mentioned the journal only when I asked directly, and had characterized its final entries as the productions of an overactive imagination under conditions of fatigue and isolation.

I kept this characterization in mind during my first week. It sustained me through the ordinary creaks and settling sounds of an old building at night, through the occasional inexplicable drop in temperature in the manuscript room, through the sense—common, I told myself, in large quiet spaces—of being observed. It did not sustain me through the night of the twenty-second, when I heard my name spoken in the darkness of the Archive's deepest vault, in a voice that knew it with the familiarity of long acquaintance, and that carried in its tone the particular warmth of something that has been waiting for a specific person and is pleased, at last, to have them arrive.
