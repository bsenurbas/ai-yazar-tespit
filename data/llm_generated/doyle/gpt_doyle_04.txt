The singular case of the blue envelope came to our notice upon a Sunday evening, when the streets were quiet and the bells of a distant church sounded faintly through the damp air. Holmes had been lying upon the settee with his pipe unlit between his teeth, a sure sign, in my experience, that his mind was at work upon some invisible problem. “Watson,” said he suddenly, “when a man writes in a lady’s hand, seals with official wax, and sends by a child, he is either very foolish or very pressed for time.” Before I could ask his meaning, Mrs. Hudson appeared with a small envelope of a deep blue shade. “For Mr. Sherlock Holmes,” said she. “Brought by a boy, sir, who ran off without waiting.”

Holmes held the envelope to the lamp. “Observe the paper, Watson. Expensive, but cut unevenly. Taken from a larger sheet. The address is written with a broad nib, yet the pressure is light; that tells us the writer tried to imitate another hand. Now the wax—red, official in appearance, but softened over a common candle, not a spirit lamp. Our correspondent wishes to borrow authority without possessing it.” He broke the seal and read aloud: “‘Come at once to Alderbrook House. The matter concerns a woman’s honour and a dead man’s watch.’” “A melodramatic summons,” said I. “A precise one,” Holmes replied. “Honour, watch, and haste: three words too carefully chosen. The sender fears exposure by time itself.”

By nine o’clock we stood in a gloomy dining-room in Kensington, where a stopped clock, a shattered glass, and an overturned chair formed the whole theatre of the affair. Holmes listened to the frightened housekeeper, questioned the footman, and then turned to the mantelpiece with a quiet laugh. “The dead man’s watch was not stolen,” said he. “It was set forward. The stopped clock was set back. Between the two lies the alibi.” He faced the young widow, who had watched him with dry eyes and clenched hands. “Madam, no one accuses you of the death, but someone has used your name to conceal the hour of it. Your brother arrived before dinner, not after. The blue envelope was his last attempt to draw me away from that fact. Watson, send for Lestrade. The case is over, though the confession remains.”
