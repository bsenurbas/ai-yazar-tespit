The adventure of the torn theatre bill began upon a wet April evening, when London shone under the gas lamps like black glass and the wheels of passing cabs hissed upon the stones. Holmes had been in one of his restless moods, pacing from mantel to window, when Mrs. Hudson brought up a gentleman who carried his hat as though he feared it might betray him. “Mr. Sherlock Holmes?” said he. “My name is Adrian Bell, and I have been accused of murdering a man I never met.” Holmes took the damp scrap of paper which our visitor offered him and smoothed it upon the table. “A theatre bill,” said he. “Torn from the Lyceum, carried in a left breast pocket, and handled by a woman before it reached you. Pray sit down, Mr. Bell. Your innocence is probable; your silence, however, has been most unwise.”

“You know I have been silent?” cried the man. “I know you have concealed your whereabouts last night. The paper is creased twice, once by you and once by someone with a smaller hand. There is face powder upon the corner, and the scent of violet water, both unfashionable in the cheaper galleries. You were not alone at the theatre. You refuse to name the lady because she is married, or because you hope she will be.” Bell turned crimson. “Her husband was found dead at midnight.” Holmes nodded. “Then we must learn whether the theatre was an alibi or a trap.” He glanced across at me. “Watson, you will observe that sentiment is the enemy of evidence, though it is often the key to motive.”

Before eleven we stood behind the stage of the Lyceum, among ropes, painted flats, and that curious smell of dust and greasepaint which belongs to the theatre alone. Holmes questioned a doorkeeper, examined a cloakroom ticket, and then stopped before a heap of discarded playbills. “Here is the answer,” said he, lifting one with his cane. “The torn half given to Mr. Bell was not from last night’s performance. It was from the previous week. The lady never entered the theatre at all; she lent him the proof of an evening she did not spend.” He turned to Bell, whose face had gone white. “You have been used, sir. The husband was dead before the curtain rose, and your gallantry supplied the murderer with an hour. Lestrade may look for the widow, but he should begin with her maid. No woman so careful with false evidence would leave powder on paper unless another hand had carried it.”
